01 / Problem

Caribbean capital is fragmented and idle.

A typical affluent household in the region holds a bond at NCB, a fund at Sagicor, cash at JMMB, and a policy or pension it has half-forgotten. Nothing talks to anything. The diaspora, which sends billions home every year, has almost no compliant way to invest into the region from abroad. The result is capital that sits in low-yield accounts because moving it is slow, opaque, and spread across institutions that each only see their own slice.

  • For the household: no single view, no advice that spans providers, and idle cash that quietly loses to inflation and FX.
  • For the diaspora: remittances flow in for consumption, not wealth creation, because cross-border onboarding and suitability are painful.
  • For the institutions: distribution is branch-bound and expensive, and they cannot see or serve the wallet a client holds elsewhere.
02 / Why now

Three curves are crossing.

Capable agents

LLM tool-use is finally good enough to research instruments, screen suitability and draft actions in plain language, at a cost that works for retail accounts.

Diaspora at scale

Remittances to the region run into the tens of billions a year. A generation abroad wants to build wealth at home, not just send money for spending.

Regional rails

FSC-licensed institutions are digitizing and opening APIs. Aggregation that was impossible five years ago is now an integration problem, not a policy one.

Read that number honestly: corridor one is proof, not the prize. US$400k ARR is a seed milestone that validates the loop (funded accounts, balance build, take rate). The venture case is what it unlocks:

  • Replication: the same playbook across the US, Canadian and UK corridors for Jamaica, Trinidad, Barbados and Guyana. Ten corridors at corridor-one scale is ~US$500M AUM and ~US$4M ARR before any growth in penetration.
  • Consolidation kicker: each diaspora account tends to pull in the household's existing domestic holdings (the bond at NCB, the fund at Sagicor). Consolidated assets arrive in large lumps and are already invested, so they convert to fee-bearing AUM immediately. This is the multiplier remittance-only models miss.
  • Deepening: penetration at 4% and balances at US$12k are entry assumptions. Mature fintech corridors reach 10%+ of segment; every point of penetration in corridor one alone is ~US$100k ARR.

Sensitivity: the model lives or dies on two inputs: penetration of the target segment and blended take rate. Remittance redirection alone does not carry it; consolidation is what moves balances from US$3k to US$12k+. That is why the seed milestone is defined as a working corridor with a measured take rate, not signups.

04 / Product

Built, not slideware.

The working product unifies holdings across partners, runs a conversational capital agent that acts inside client-set limits, escalates larger moves for one-tap approval, and gives each partner its own console for the order flow it receives. Approving a deal on the client side routes a real order into the partner side in the same session.

  • Client app: unified portfolio, agent, opportunities marketplace, cross-border planning, KYC onboarding.
  • Partner console: a separate surface where institutions accept and settle routed orders, manage listings, and see referred-client KYC.
  • Guardrails: the agent proposes; a server-side limits engine and human approval are the only paths to execution, with an immutable audit trail.

06 / Moat

The wedge is easy. The compounding is the moat.

Partner integrations

Each licensed integration is slow and relationship-heavy, which is exactly why a network of them is hard to copy. First mover on the shelf sets the default.

Two-sided network

More partners mean better agent matches, which bring more investors, which bring partners more AUM, which attracts more partners. Neutral aggregation is a position no single incumbent can occupy.

Regulatory posture

Being the orchestration layer that never touches custody or execution is a deliberate, defensible position that keeps us light while partners carry the licensed duties.

Data and trust

Cross-partner holdings, suitability and outcomes give the agent context no single institution has, and the audit trail earns the trust that lets clients grant real limits.

We are not claiming a moat today. We are claiming a credible path to one, and the first integration plus the first corridor are what turn the claim into evidence.

07 / Regulatory strategy
The question every serious investor asks first

We orchestrate. Partners are regulated.

An agent that recommends and routes orders can constitute regulated activity, and we treat that as the central execution risk rather than hand-waving it. Our position: CCN is an introducer and technology layer. Custody, execution, suitability sign-off and KYC/AML remain with the FSC-licensed partner on every transaction. The agent surfaces and prepares; a licensed entity is always the actor of record.

  • Per-jurisdiction: we scope the licensing path (introducer vs advisory) with counsel in each market before we route a single order there.
  • Consent-based KYC: we link a partner's existing verification with client consent; we do not become the KYC owner.
  • Auditability: every proposed, approved and executed action is logged immutably, so a regulator or partner can reconstruct any decision.
  • Data residency: client data is held in-region.
08 / Go to market

Solve the cold start with one corridor and one anchor.

Two-sided products die trying to boil the ocean. We do not launch eight partners and every diaspora at once. We land one anchor institution that wants distribution and one diaspora corridor (for example, the UK or US Jamaican community) and make that single loop work end to end before widening. Supply and demand are seeded together, in one market, deliberately narrow.

Beachhead

One corridor, one anchor partner, one flagship instrument set. Prove the routed-order loop and the take rate.

Expand supply

Add partners onto proven demand. Each new listing improves agent matches for the users already there.

Expand corridors

Replicate the loop into the next diaspora market once CAC and retention hold in the first.

09 / Competition

Everyone owns a slice. Nobody owns the account.

  • Incumbent apps (NCB, JMMB, Sagicor): excellent for their own products, structurally unable to show or advise on a competitor's. They cannot be the neutral layer.
  • Remittance and neobank apps: move money well, but stop at the wallet. No invested products, no suitability, no regional custody.
  • Generic aggregators / robo-advisors: built for US/UK rails and products; they do not have the Caribbean partner relationships or the diaspora corridor focus.

CCN wins by being the one place that is neutral across partners, agent-native, and built for the region-plus-diaspora reality rather than retrofitted to it.

12 / Key risks

What could sink this.

Regulatory

A jurisdiction could deem the agent to be giving regulated advice. Mitigation: introducer posture, counsel per market, licensed partner as actor of record.

Partner dependence

Early reliance on one or two anchors. Mitigation: neutral positioning and fast follow-on integrations once demand is proven.

Cold start

Two-sided liquidity is hard. Mitigation: one narrow corridor seeded on both sides before expansion.

Take rate

Blended economics are unproven. Mitigation: this is precisely what the seed milestone validates before scaling spend.
